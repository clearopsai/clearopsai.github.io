The fifteen days

What actually happens, day by day.

Two scheduled calls, and no production credentials at any point. Everything else happens on my time, not yours.

01

Days 1 to 4

We pick the one workflow and, if there is one, the incident worth reconstructing. Then a forty-five minute session with the engineer who owns the integration. We draw the path across the top of a grid: every point where the transaction changes hands, and who owns each side of it.

02

Days 5 to 12

The awkward part. At every handoff, the same three questions: what is supposed to happen here, what record exists that it did, and where does that record live. Then I go and look at the record itself. Status is one of three words. Proven, partial, absent.

03

Days 13 to 15

The report lands in your hands before the readout call, not after it. It names the handoff where the evidence stops proving continuation, and gives you checks you can run afterwards without me. Then a call to walk it.

What I look for

Six ways a record stops proving what it looks like it proves.

A decline from the partner and a timeout your own gateway synthesised are written to the same log in the same format. When the log says declined, what proves the partner actually answered?
The synthesised answer
The handoff is proven on the way out. Nothing independent proves it arrived. Who holds the record that it landed, you or them?
The one-sided proof
A retry and a first attempt look identical once the day is over. If the same transaction appears twice, which one was real?
The indistinguishable retry
The only record that a handoff happened lives in the other party's system. When they say they never got it, what can you show?
The record you do not hold
An incident took hours to settle and the finding was that nobody could prove where it stopped. What was the last thing you could prove, and how long did that take to establish?
The six-hour investigation
The logging change that would fix it has been in the backlog since the last incident. What is in the backlog because an investigation was painful?
